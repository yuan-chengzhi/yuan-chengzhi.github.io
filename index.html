Mathematics · Artificial Intelligence · AI4Math

Chengzhi Yuan 袁诚志

I study how AI systems can organize, verify, preserve, and continue complex mathematical work across long horizons—starting with formal proof, tools, shared state, and evidence.

My work began with a practical question: how can an agent make reliable progress on a difficult mathematical problem? As the tasks became longer and more dependent, the question became broader: what must a research environment preserve so that partial work can be checked, handed off, and continued? Formal mathematics is my current setting; the broader question concerns how tools, evidence, and organizations should change as the work scales.

Putnam 2025

2025 · early experiment

Question

How can tools, structured feedback, and an execution loop help an agent make reliable progress on a difficult mathematical task?

My contribution

I worked on the Pi Math First / Pi Lean Tools environment, prompt design, and the Ralph-style execution loop, then examined what the runs suggested about the next useful system improvements.

Evidence & boundary

Internal experiment note. Twelve problems were run with eight parallel attempts per problem; one full run took about 5.5 hours, and all twelve reached the apparatus-defined internal `final_verified` state. This is not an official Putnam score or a claim about general mathematical ability.

04 · Research horizon

What may change when the work scales

The projects above are the evidence I have so far. The directions below are hypotheses and open questions, not completed systems or predictions I want to treat as facts.

AI4Math · open hypothesis

From proof generation to mathematical ecosystems

When proofs become cheap to produce, who—or what—decides which work should be verified, understood, reused, or pursued?

As formal proof production becomes more abundant, correctness remains necessary but may no longer be the hardest bottleneck. Mathematical context, understanding, value, transfer, and reuse may become equally important.

verificationunderstandingvalue
Multi-agent systems · open hypothesis

From agent coordination to evolving organizations

When many agents share a difficult task, how can the system discover and revise its communities, routes, protocols, and resource allocation?

As the number of agents grows, the central problem may move beyond assigning tasks. Organization, information flow, communication, and the division between local and global decisions may themselves become objects of experiment.

organizationinformation flowcoordination
Common problem · working direction

Scaling evidence and state

How can a system preserve enough structure, provenance, and feedback for work to remain checkable and useful as tasks, agents, and artifacts multiply?

This is the bridge between the two horizons and the projects already recorded here: local reasoning only becomes cumulative when a system can retain, evaluate, and organize what happened.
